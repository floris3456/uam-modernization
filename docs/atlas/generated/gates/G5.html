What are we proving?

Prove a crash or retry cannot move progress ahead of durable effects, lose an event, or change its identity.

Why now?

The endpoint must survive disconnection and interruption before any server custody claim is meaningful.

What depends on it?

  • Release, identity, diagnostics, and server-custody integration proofs.

Preparation that may happen earlier

  • Model SQLite states, faults, batches, receipts, pressure, and migrations with fictional data.

Work allowed

  • Fictional state-machine and SQLite experiments after G0; integration only after G3 and G4.

Work prohibited

  • Advance a cursor without the matching durable effect.
  • Rebuild an ambiguous batch or delete before a valid receipt.

What must humans decide?

  • Approve endpoint data-at-rest and operational pressure policy after measurements.

What must be measured with CLI evidence?

  • Crash-point enumeration, model comparison, power-loss/corruption simulation, sealed retry identity, migration, pressure, and receipt cleanup tests.

What evidence is required?

  • State-transition ledger, SQLite settings and version, fault schedule, page/batch digests, receipt proofs, and recovery traces.

What causes failure or hold?

Stop on cursor-ahead, missing or double effect, changed retry identity, silent loss, cleanup before valid receipt, or corrupt/mixed state accepted.

Failure, reproduction, cleanup, and recovery

  • Stop the writer, preserve the fictional database and fault point, restore the last proven state, and replay the same sealed bytes.

What does passing unlock?

  • Release, identity, diagnostics, and server-custody integration proofs.

What passing never authorizes

  • Production retention or capacity claims.

Pass condition

All enumerated crashes and retries preserve one durable effect per event, correct progress, identical retry bytes, valid receipt cleanup, and recoverable state.
